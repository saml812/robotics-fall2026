A suitable route for a robot sharing space with people has to be good at three levels. It must be found efficiently; it must be easy for the robot to actually drive, and it must leave people enough room. 

Search efficiency and route quality. BFS and A* both found the shortest routes (13.5 m open, 24.5 m constrained). A* examined fewer cells on the open map (700 vs 923) because its Manhattan heuristic guided the order. DFS was cheap to compute (448 expansions on the constrained map) but returned a 50.0 m route. IDDFS guaranteed short routes in principle but hit the 250,000-expansion limit on both larger maps. That resource_limit is an unfinished search, which means it did not find a route. So efficiency only helps if it doesn’t cost route quality. A* got both, DFS traded quality for speed, and IDDFS gave up efficiency without delivering a route.

Navigation on both maps. Planning time was fairly short, so route quality impacted most of the journey. BFS and A* took about 46 s (open) and 90 s (constrained). The smoothed, cost-aware Nav2 Smac route took 36 s and 73 s. DFS failed on both maps: on the constrained map it stopped after 146 s and 31.8 m, with the controller detecting a collision ahead at a tight turn beside a wall. This shows that fewer expansions do not mean a better journey. A route that was created with fewer expansions can have a lot of sharp turns, which means that the robot has to slow down to follow the path. Compared to routes that have fewer tight turns, but may take more expansions to obtain. The robot doesn't have to slow down as much when following the path and therefore, it can reach the goal a lot faster.

Social trade-offs. The stronger field (repulsion 1.3, influence 1.5 m) raised A*'s closest person clearance from 0.76 m to 0.84 m at no measurable time cost. The weaker setting entered personal space for 1.8 s. The field only helps when the route passes inside its influence distance (RRT already stayed 1.7 m away), and it can oscillate in narrow passages. So the best route for shared space is a good search result, refined for both drivability and personal space, and checked on the robot, because a plan that looks good does not guarantee a good journey.

Estimates vs. ground truth. The planned lengths and the grid are exact for the model, but executed distance, clearance, and goal error come from AMCL estimates and lidar proxies. Each cell was run once, so the success of each route is not guaranteed.
