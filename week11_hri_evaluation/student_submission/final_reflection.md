What stood out to me most was how error-prone the speech recognizer was. "Confirm" was misheard several times, and a vague request was once turned into a specific cup. A number of times, the microphone couldn't pick up audio properly, or the voice recognizer failed, making the task take longer to complete. Writing the redesign made me realize that good feedback is a safety feature. Telling the user exactly what the robot heard, and that it will not move yet, gives them a chance to catch errors before anything happens.

This lab made me more interested in human-robot interaction and assistive robotics. Getting a controller to pass its checks is only part of the work, the system also has to be understandable and correctable by the person it is meant to help. I want to keep working on problems where the technical design and the human experience have to be considered together.
