For a routine handover I would choose autonomous execution. In my trials it delivered the correct cup in 60.2 s with only 2 operator inputs, compared with 162.3 s and 6 inputs for shared autonomy with voice and 182.1 s and 114 keyboard inputs for manual teleoperation. When the goal is known in advance and does not change, the person only needs to start the task, so autonomy keeps their effort lowest and avoids problems from speech recognition.

When the person's needs may change mid-task, I would choose shared autonomy with voice. Autonomous mode handled the changed goal (62.3 s, 4 inputs) only because I stopped it at the checkpoint and re-set the goal to red through the interface; the person being served has no direct way to redirect it. Manual teleoperation also delivered the red cup (133.2 s, 61 inputs), but it needed constant attention and precise docking within 0.16 m of each station, which is a heavy load for someone who needs assistance. Shared voice was the slowest on the changed goal (185.2 s, 11 inputs) because of recording and confirmation steps, but it let the person state the red-cup correction in their own words, and the controller only switched tasks after an explicit confirmation, so the change was both easy to make and safe.

My voice redesign changed the ambiguous-request interaction from Mission 2. In the baseline, "bring me the cup" was misheard as "the red cup", so a request that named no cup became a red-cup goal. In the matched retest, after a few unrecognized recordings, the vague request went to CLARIFY. Saying "the blue cup" moved to CONFIRM with my new message: "I heard the blue cup. I will not move until you say confirm. Wrong cup? Say the red cup or the blue cup." The robot waited until "confirm" was heard and then delivered the correct cup.
