# pillar_search

## diagnosis
Successful centering: 20261031T000820-137e4422 (blue only, gain 1.2, tolerance 0.10, hold 0.7 s). My prediction was that a blue pillar on the left of the image gives a negative error, so the controller command -gain x error is positive and the robot turns left. That is what happened. The timeline shows SEARCH for 144 steps (no blue in the processing region, slow search rotation), then CENTER 13 steps, CONFIRM 2, HOLD 7, COMPLETE. When blue first appeared, the white blob in the mask was at the left side of the cyan processing region, so the yellow centroid was left of the magenta center and the error plot starts near -1. The turn-rate plot stays positive (0 to about 0.45 rad/s) the whole run, so the robot kept turning left and the error shrank toward 0 until it was inside the 0.10 tolerance. Forward speed stayed 0. Mean |error| while visible was 0.378 over 16.6 s of simulation time.

Setting change: I changed only the steering gain from 1.2 to 2.4 and compared the two four-color attempts that started from the same pose (both started facing the blue pillar): 20261031T001016-ad4efd3e (gain 1.2) and 20261031T001537-c9fea784 (gain 2.4). Doubling the gain did not make the task faster (21.5 s vs 21.4 s) or more accurate (mean |error| 0.347 vs 0.354), because most of the time is spent in the fixed-speed SEARCH state, not in CENTER. It made centering less stable: at gain 2.4 the turn rate went negative (down to about -0.3 rad/s), meaning the robot overshot past center and had to turn back right, and on turquoise the timeline went CENTER -> CONFIRM -> back to CENTER -> CONFIRM before it could HOLD. At gain 1.2 the turn rate never went negative and every color went straight from CENTER to CONFIRM to HOLD. So the higher gain worsened stability with no benefit, and 1.2 was the better setting. (Run 20261031T001337-0b69e323 was a blue-only run at gain 2.4 that finished almost instantly because the robot was already centered on blue from the previous run, so it is not a fair comparison.)

## evidence
Four-color attempt 20261031T001016-ad4efd3e (red, green, turquoise, blue; gain 1.2): all four colors were centered and held, and the outcome was complete in 21.5 s of simulation time, with visited_colors = red, green, turquoise, blue and forward speed 0 throughout. None were missed.

State timeline: WAIT_CAMERA x3 at the start (the controller refused to move until fresh frames arrived), then for each color the same pattern: red SEARCH 32 -> CENTER 12 -> CONFIRM 3 -> HOLD 8 -> NEXT; green SEARCH 29 -> CENTER 13 -> CONFIRM 3 -> HOLD 7 -> NEXT; turquoise SEARCH 30 -> CENTER 14 -> CONFIRM 2 -> HOLD 7 -> NEXT; blue SEARCH 30 -> CENTER 13 -> CONFIRM 2 -> HOLD 7 -> COMPLETE. The similar SEARCH lengths fit four pillars spaced roughly evenly around the robot, each needing about a quarter turn at the search speed. In the camera evidence the mask only shows the requested color inside the processing region, the yellow centroid moves toward the magenta center during CENTER, and the error plot drops to near 0 before CONFIRM and HOLD. Mean |error| while a target was visible was 0.347 (normalized image units, not metres).

Why this is not proof of identity or distance: the controller only knows that a large enough connected region of pixels fell inside an HSV range and that its centroid was near the image center for a few frames. Any object of the same color (a turquoise wall, a poster, another robot) would produce the same mask and the same HOLD. A colored region is not the same as a pillar. The error is a horizontal image coordinate, so centering only tells us bearing (the pillar is straight ahead), not how far away it is: a small near object and a big far one can look the same. A stable hold just means the image did not change while the robot stood still. Distance would need depth, a known object size with camera calibration, or another sensor.
