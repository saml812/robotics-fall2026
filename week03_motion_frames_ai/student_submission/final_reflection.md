# Final reflection

Respond to any or all of these prompts:

1. What did this activity make you think about regarding your own interests in robotics, computing, engineering, or related work?
2. How did this activity affect your motivation to do similar kinds of work in the future?
3. What value do you see in connecting technical or computing work with human, ethical, or societal considerations?
4. What stood out to you about the activity, and why?
5. Is there anything else you would like to share about your experience with the activity?

## Response

This activity made me think about how robotics is never just about code. I enjoyed the precision of specifying arcs, deriving speeds, and writing tests, but the sociotechnical question was the most memorable. It reminded me that a robot’s success depends on how people interpret its motion, not just on whether its odometry is accurate. Connecting technical work with human and ethical considerations is important otherwise, we build systems that are correct in isolation but fail in context. This activity reinforces that engineering decisions are also social decisions, and that testing should include people, not just parameters.
